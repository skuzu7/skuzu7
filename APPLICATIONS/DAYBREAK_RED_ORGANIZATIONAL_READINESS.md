# OpenAI Daybreak Red — organizational readiness (public, non-attestation)

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** future organizational-readiness planning. This document is **not** evidence of Daybreak Red approval, a completed KYB, security-control implementation, or a qualifying business.

## Decision gate

The [official Daybreak overview](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001258-openai-daybreak-trusted-access-for-cyber-overview) states that **individuals can apply for Blue**, while **Red is for approved business and enterprise organizations**. The direct individual application does not accept Red requests. Individual exceptions may exist, but are not an application path.

Approval of Daybreak Blue does **not** grant Red. Daybreak Red access also does **not** automatically grant every cyber-specific model: GPT-6 Cyber requires additional approval and setup. Approved access is only for lawful, authorized internal security work, not resale or downstream exposure to customers.

**Public portfolio status:** [CSA-LAB](https://github.com/skuzu7/CSA-LAB), [TRACEGUARD](https://github.com/skuzu7/CSA-LAB/tree/main/agent_replay_lab), [freeband-nextjs](https://github.com/skuzu7/freeband-nextjs) show first-party and synthetic engineering. They do **not** establish organizational eligibility, external vulnerability attribution, customer authorization or program status.

## Organization application evidence gates

| Gate | What a reviewer should be able to verify | Status of publicly available portfolio evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal identity | Registered business/enterprise identity and authorized representative (in provider's private KYB process) | **Not verified** |
| Legitimate cyber capability | Team roles, genuine engineering/research deliverables, externally verifiable qualifications if available | **Partially documented only through first-party research** |
| Authorized Red use case | Named internal systems or authorized targets; proposed workflow, written scope and why Blue is insufficient | **Not verified for an organization** |
| Account and workspace | Which OpenAI business/enterprise organization, workspace and API projects will be used | **Not verified** |
| Security governance | Named owner, access review, approved users, key policy, MFA/SSO or other requirements as directed in onboarding | **Not verified** |
| Handling sensitive work | Data classification, retention agreement, logging, incident response, customer-data separation | **Not verified** |
| Provider acceptance | Official KYB / application decision and tier enablement | **Not publicly established** |
| GPT-6 Cyber access | Separate provider approval, IP allowlisting and any applicable Private Safety Processing | **Not publicly established** |

Requirements may differ by deployment and evolve. The provider's business onboarding flow controls eligibility; this matrix is a preparation tool, not a substitute.

## Intended future use-case outline (not a claim of activity)

- Authorized, bounded vulnerability research on owned or contractually permitted code and laboratory assets.
- Reproducible triage, minimal proof-of-concept validation and root-cause analysis.
- Human-reviewed remediation and regression verification.
- Detection content and controlled purple-team evaluation.
- Clear Rules of Engagement: target ownership, exclusions, testing windows, rate/impact limits, stop conditions, escalation, retention and disclosure.

No customer-facing feature, proxy access or shared seat will expose Daybreak Red without the provider's separate approval.

## Claims not to make until independently verified

- "Daybreak Red approved," "GPT-6 Cyber enabled," "enterprise verified," or "KYB completed."
- "Our team" when only personal contributions are documented.
- "Production zero-day discoveries" or "paid pentesting clients" based on synthetic CSA-LAB results.
- "All security controls implemented" based only on policy documents.

## Acceptance steps — actual evidence, not marketing

1. Determine the actual legal entity and authorized application representative.
2. Identify a genuine internal cybersecurity need beyond Blue and a permitted system scope.
3. Collect independently verifiable team/research evidence and authorized activity records.
4. Establish organization controls and confirm configuration privately; do not post credentials, network topology, passports or customer files publicly.
5. Apply through the [official organizational route](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001258-openai-daybreak-trusted-access-for-cyber-overview), complete verification, and record the decision privately.
6. If separately approved for GPT-6 Cyber, complete model-specific requirements; Red alone is not enough.
7. Recheck current policies immediately before submission.
